Planned techniques#

The methods below are not yet available in catsim; each one’s specification on GitHub gives its definition, parameters and sources.

Technique

Family

Motivation

Key reference

Spec

Owen sequential Bayesian estimator

estimation

Reproduces Owen’s procedure, which carries only the mean and variance of a normal approximation of the posterior from one response to the next, and shows how that approximation and the order of the responses move the ability estimate away from the full posterior’s estimate

[Owen, 1975]

owen-estimator.md

Posterior Kullback-Leibler selector (KLP)

selection

Weights the KL divergence by the current posterior, not by the KL selector’s uniform window, so selection is broad early in the test and concentrates as the posterior narrows, with no half-width schedule to tune

[Chang and Ying, 1996]

klp-selector.md

Minimum expected posterior variance selector (MEPV)

selection

Chooses the item that minimizes the expected posterior variance after the next response, which targets squared-error loss directly; its gains over MEI and MPWI are modest

[van der Linden, 1998]; [Choi and Swartz, 2009]

mepv-selector.md

Maximum posterior weighted information selector (MPWI)

selection

Weights each item’s information by the current posterior: the Bayesian counterpart of MLWI, whose prior can carry what is known about the examinee in advance

[van der Linden, 1998]

mpwi-selector.md

Sympson-Hetter exposure control

exposure control

Accepts the item the selector proposes with a probability calibrated by simulation, so that each item’s exposure rate stays at about the exposure cap \(r_{\max}\) or below; the mechanism works with any selector, but its probabilities are calibrated for one selector, test design and population

[Sympson and Hetter, 1985]; [Stocking and Lewis, 1998]

sympson-hetter-exposure.md

Sequential probability ratio test stopper (SPRT)

stopping

Classifies the examinee above or below a cut score as soon as the likelihood ratio of the responses at two abilities around the cut crosses Wald’s bounds, deciding from the likelihood where the confidence-interval stopper decides from the ability estimate and its standard error

[Reckase, 1983]; [Wald, 1947]

sprt-stopper.md

Generalized likelihood ratio stopper (GLR)

stopping

Compares the likelihood at the maximum likelihood estimate with that at the nearest point of the competing hypothesis, not at the SPRT’s two fixed points; in Bartroff et al.’s simulations, its classification tests were nearly as powerful as a truncated SPRT’s and considerably shorter

[Bartroff et al., 2008]

glr-stopper.md

Stochastic curtailment stopper

stopping

Stops a truncated SPRT once its classification is unlikely to change before the maximum test length, so it never administers more items than the SPRT; in Finkelman’s simulation, it shortened the tests of examinees near the cut score with virtually no loss of accuracy

[Finkelman, 2008]; [Finkelman, 2010]

stochastic-curtailment-stopper.md

Kullback-Leibler information stopper

stopping

Stops when no remaining item has enough integrated KL information around the ability estimate, so that stopping uses the same scale as KL item selection; no source proposes this rule, which applies the minimum-information rule to Chang and Ying’s KL index

[Chang and Ying, 1996]; [Choi et al., 2011]

kl-stopper.md

Minimum information in bank stopper

stopping

Stops when no remaining item is informative enough at the ability estimate, which shortens the tests of examinees at extreme abilities, for whom the item bank has few informative items; alone it typically gives less precise estimates than a standard error stopper, and Choi et al. combine the two

[Dodd et al., 1989]; [Choi et al., 2011]

min-info-bank-stopper.md

Predicted standard error reduction stopper (PSER)

stopping

Predicts how much the best remaining item would reduce the standard error, stops before the target when that reduction is negligible, and continues past the target while it is still substantial

[Choi et al., 2011]

predicted-sem-stopper.md
