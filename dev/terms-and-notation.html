Terms and Notation#

The catsim documentation uses one term for each concept. This page lists each term with the synonyms you may know from the literature and the name to look for in the API, then the symbols that the formulas use.

Examinees and items#

examinee

The person who takes a test. Also called respondent, test taker or candidate.

ability \(\theta\)

The latent trait a test measures, on a continuous scale. Also called proficiency, latent trait or person parameter. A simulation knows each examinee’s true ability: the true_thetas it is run with.

ability estimate \(\hat{\theta}\)

The estimate of an examinee’s ability from the responses so far: provisional while the test runs, final when it ends. In the API: theta in a strategy’s context, current_theta on a session, final_thetas in a simulation result.

item

One question of a test, scored correct or incorrect. In the API an item is its row in the item bank, counted from 0.

item bank

The calibrated items a test draws from. Also called item pool. In the API: ItemBank, which holds \(M\) items, n_items.

logistic models

The item response models catsim supports: the one-, two-, three- and four-parameter logistic models (1PL to 4PL). The 1PL with every discrimination equal to 1 is the Rasch model. ItemBank.model tells which of them a bank’s parameters need.

discrimination \(a_i\)

How sharply item \(i\) separates examinees of lower and higher ability: the slope of its characteristic curve. Also called the slope parameter. ItemBank.discrimination.

difficulty \(b_i\)

The ability at which item \(i\)’s characteristic curve is steepest, on the same scale as \(\theta\). Also called the location or threshold. ItemBank.difficulty.

guessing parameter \(c_i\)

The lower asymptote of item \(i\)’s characteristic curve: the probability that an examinee of very low ability answers correctly. Also called the pseudo-guessing parameter. ItemBank.pseudo_guessing; 0 under the 1PL and 2PL.

upper asymptote \(d_i\)

The upper asymptote of item \(i\)’s characteristic curve: the probability that an examinee of very high ability answers correctly. \(1 - d_i\) is called the slipping or carelessness parameter. ItemBank.upper_asymptote; 1 under every model but the 4PL.

item characteristic curve \(P_i(\theta)\)

The probability of a correct response to item \(i\) at ability \(\theta\). Also called the item response function (IRF). ItemBank.icc.

item information \(I_i(\theta)\)

The Fisher information of item \(i\) at ability \(\theta\): how much a response to the item sharpens an estimate near \(\theta\). ItemBank.information.

test information \(I(\theta)\)

The sum of the information of the administered items. The more information, the smaller the standard error. The functions for these quantities are in the catsim.irt module.

The adaptive test#

response \(u_i\)

The examinee’s answer to item \(i\), scored correct (\(u_i = 1\), True in the API) or incorrect (\(u_i = 0\), False).

responses \(\mathbf{u}\)

The responses to the administered items, in the order they were given. Also called a response pattern or response vector. In the API: responses.

administered items

The items given so far, in order. In the API: administered_items.

test length \(n\)

The number of administered items. In the API: test_lengths in a simulation result. A MaxLengthStopper ends the test at max_items; a selector that plans its schedule for a test of known length takes it as test_size.

session

One examinee’s test, from the first item to the stop. Its state is a CatSessionState.

simulation

A batch of sessions, one for each true ability, run by a SimulationRunner, which returns a SimulationResult.

initializer

The strategy that sets the ability estimate before the first response, the starting point or initial ability estimate of the literature.

selector

The strategy that chooses the next item. Also called the item selection rule or item selection criterion.

estimator

The strategy that estimates ability from the responses, such as maximum likelihood (ML), expected a posteriori (EAP), maximum a posteriori (MAP) or Warm’s weighted likelihood (WLE). Also called the ability estimation method.

stopper

The strategy that decides whether the test ends: a stopping criterion. Also called a stopping rule or termination criterion. Every stopper derives from BaseStopper, for example MaxLengthStopper or StandardErrorStopper. StopRule is the stopper that combines several others; these pages say “rule” only for it.

stop reason

Why a session ended: StopReason.STOPPER when its stopper was satisfied, StopReason.NO_ITEMS when its selector had no item to offer.

standard error \(SE(\hat{\theta})\)

The uncertainty of an ability estimate, on the scale of \(\theta\). Also called the standard error of estimation (SEE) or standard error of measurement (SEM). Each estimator computes it by its own formula; for maximum likelihood it is \(1/\sqrt{I(\hat{\theta})}\). In the API: se in an Estimate and in a strategy’s context, current_se on a session, final_ses in a simulation result.

Simulation results#

exposure rate \(r_i\)

The share of examinees who were given item \(i\). In the API: exposure_rates in a simulation result. Exposure-control selectors limit it with a cap, r_max. While a run is in progress the rates are counted over the examinees tested so far; ExposureTracker says how.

overlap rate

The expected share of items that two examinees have in common. In the API: overlap_rate in a simulation result, None unless every session has the same test length.

bias, MSE, RMSE

How far the final estimates are from the true abilities, over the examinees: the mean of \(\hat{\theta} - \theta\), the mean of \((\hat{\theta} - \theta)^2\), and its square root. A positive bias means abilities are overestimated. In the API: bias, mse and rmse in a simulation result.
