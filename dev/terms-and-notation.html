Terms and Notation#

The catsim documentation uses one term for each concept. This page lists each term with the synonyms you may know from the literature and the name to look for in the API, then the symbols that the formulas use.

Examinees and items#

examinee

The person who takes a test. Also called respondent or test taker.

ability \(\theta\)

The latent trait a test measures, on a continuous scale. Also called proficiency, latent trait or person parameter. A simulation knows each examinee’s true ability when it is run with true_thetas.

ability estimate \(\hat{\theta}\)

The estimate of an examinee’s ability from the responses so far: provisional while the test runs, final when it ends. In the API: theta in an Estimate and in a strategy’s context, current_theta on a session, the theta column of a simulation result’s tables.

item

One question of a test, scored correct or incorrect. In the API an item is its row in the item bank, its index, counted from 0 (item). Each item also has a name, unique in its bank (name), which results, plots and error messages show.

item bank

The calibrated items a test draws from. Also called item pool. In the API: ItemBank, which holds \(M\) items, n_items, and their names.

logistic models

The item response models catsim supports: the one-, two-, three- and four-parameter logistic models (1PL to 4PL). The 1PL with every discrimination equal to 1 is the Rasch model. ItemBank.model tells which of them a bank’s parameters need.

discrimination \(a_i\)

How sharply item \(i\) separates examinees of lower and higher ability: the slope of its characteristic curve. Also called the slope parameter. ItemBank.discrimination.

difficulty \(b_i\)

The ability at which item \(i\)’s characteristic curve is steepest, on the same scale as \(\theta\). Also called the location or threshold. ItemBank.difficulty.

guessing parameter \(c_i\)

The lower asymptote of item \(i\)’s characteristic curve: the probability that an examinee of very low ability answers correctly. Also called the pseudo-guessing parameter. ItemBank.pseudo_guessing; 0 under the 1PL and 2PL.

upper asymptote \(d_i\)

The upper asymptote of item \(i\)’s characteristic curve: the probability that an examinee of very high ability answers correctly. \(1 - d_i\) is called the slipping or carelessness parameter. ItemBank.upper_asymptote; 1 under every model but the 4PL.

item characteristic curve \(P_i(\theta)\)

The probability of a correct response to item \(i\) at ability \(\theta\). Also called the item response function (IRF). ItemBank.icc.

item information \(I_i(\theta)\)

The Fisher information of item \(i\) at ability \(\theta\): how much a response to the item sharpens an estimate near \(\theta\). ItemBank.information.

test information \(I(\theta)\)

The sum of the information of the administered items. The more information, the smaller the standard error. The functions for these quantities are in irtlib’s irtlib.irt module.

The adaptive test#

response \(u_i\)

The examinee’s answer to item \(i\), scored correct (\(u_i = 1\), True or 1 in the API) or incorrect (\(u_i = 0\), False or 0).

responses \(\mathbf{u}\)

The responses to the administered items, in the order they were given. Also called a response pattern or response vector. In the API: responses.

administered items

The items given so far, in order. In the API: administered_items.

excluded items

Items a session must not be given although it has not had them, such as the items its exposure control declined. They do not count toward the test length. In the API: excluded_items.

candidate items

The items a selector may still choose: those neither administered nor excluded. A selector may narrow them further, for example to the items below its exposure cap. In these pages a candidate is always an item, never an examinee. In the API: the candidates of the selection helpers.

test length \(n\)

The number of administered items. In the API: the test_length column and metric of a simulation result. A MaxLengthStopper ends the test at max_items; a selector that plans its schedule for a test of known length takes it as test_size.

session

One examinee’s test, from the initial estimate to the stop. In the API: Session, the record of the test so far, which each step of CatEngine returns anew.

initializer

The strategy that sets the ability estimate before the first response, the starting point or initial ability estimate of the literature.

selector

The strategy that chooses the next item. Also called the item selection rule or item selection criterion.

estimator

The strategy that estimates ability from the responses, such as maximum likelihood (ML), expected a posteriori (EAP), maximum a posteriori (MAP) or Warm’s weighted likelihood (WLE). Also called the ability estimation method.

stopper

The strategy that decides whether the test ends: a stopping criterion. Also called a stopping rule or termination criterion. Every stopper derives from BaseStopper, for example MaxLengthStopper or StandardErrorStopper. StopRule is the stopper that combines several others; these pages say “rule” only for it.

exposure control

Keeping items from being given so often that they become known to future examinees. Some selectors do it themselves, with an exposure cap r_max or by choosing at random. An engine can also take an exposure control, a BaseExposureControl, which may decline the item the selector proposes; the session then excludes that item for the rest of its test.

stop reason

Why a session ended: StopReason.STOPPER when its stopper was satisfied, StopReason.NO_ITEMS when its selector had no item to offer, or none that the exposure control accepted.

standard error \(SE(\hat{\theta})\)

The uncertainty of an ability estimate, on the scale of \(\theta\). Also called the standard error of estimation (SEE) or standard error of measurement (SEM). Each estimator computes it by its own formula; for maximum likelihood it is \(1/\sqrt{I(\hat{\theta})}\). In the API: se in an Estimate and in a strategy’s context, current_se on a session, the se column of a simulation result’s tables.

Simulations#

simulation

A run of one session per examinee, by simulate, which returns a SimulationResult. A run is one condition; a study compares several.

generated examinee

A simulated examinee given by a true ability, who answers each item correctly with probability \(P_i(\theta)\). In the API: an entry of true_thetas.

recorded examinee

A simulated examinee whose answers come from data, as in a post-hoc study of a test given linearly. In the API: a row of the response matrix responses, one column per item of the bank, with NaN where an answer is missing.

replication

One of several runs of the same condition with other seeds, which together show how much its metrics vary.

common random numbers

Running the conditions a study compares on the same random draws, so that their difference carries less Monte Carlo error. Run on the same seed, catsim’s simulated examinees give every engine the same answer to each item.

part, worker

A part is a contiguous share of a simulation’s examinees, whose sessions run in order with exposure counts of their own (parts). A worker is a process that runs parts (workers).

exposure rate \(r_i\)

The share of examinees who were given item \(i\). In the API: exposure_rates() of a simulation result, and the exposure_rates a selector is given at each step. Exposure-control selectors limit it with a cap, r_max. simulate gives the rule for the rates each session sees.

exposure feedback

The dependence of a session on the sessions before it through the exposure rates it sees, under a selector’s r_max cap.

proposal rate

The share of examinees for whom the selector proposed item \(i\): the item was given or the exposure control declined it. In the API: proposal_rates() of a simulation result. Without an exposure control it equals the exposure rate.

overlap rate

The expected share of items that two examinees have in common. In the API: overlap_rate() of a simulation result, None unless every session has the same test length.

bias, MSE, RMSE

How far the final estimates are from the true abilities, over the examinees: the mean of \(\hat{\theta} - \theta\), the mean of \((\hat{\theta} - \theta)^2\), and its square root. A positive bias means abilities are overestimated. In the API: bias(), mse() and rmse() of a simulation result.

Monte Carlo standard error

How much a metric would vary over replications: the uncertainty that the finite number of simulated examinees leaves in it. Each metric of a simulation result comes with it, as mc_se.

decile

One tenth of a simulation’s examinees by true ability, from decile 0, the lowest abilities, to 9. In the API: deciles() of a simulation result, and the decile argument of its metrics.
