Expected A Posteriori Estimator#

Class:

catsim.estimation.EAPEstimator

Sources:

Bock and Mislevy [1982]; Bock and Aitkin [1981]

Motivation#

EAP estimates ability by taking the mean of the posterior distribution rather than the maximizer of a likelihood. In CAT practice this is attractive because it always returns a finite value, even for all-correct or all-incorrect patterns where the MLE does not exist, so no heuristic such as Dodd’s is needed early in the test; Bock and Mislevy [1982, p. 443] conclude that it is stable at all adaptive test lengths, including the first few items.

EAP’s posterior also yields the posterior variance at no extra cost, and that variance serves as the error variance of the estimate [Bock and Mislevy, 1982, p. 432]; in their simulations the posterior standard deviation behaved like the standard error of measurement (p. 431). The estimator reports the posterior standard deviation as the estimate’s standard error [Magis and Raîche, 2012, p. 6, Equation 11], and the precision stoppers judge it.

Definition#

The EAP estimate is the posterior mean:

\[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAP}} = \mathbb{E}[\theta \mid \mathbf{u}] = \frac{\int \theta\,p(\theta)\,L(\mathbf{u}\mid\theta)\,d\theta} {\int p(\theta)\,L(\mathbf{u}\mid\theta)\,d\theta}.\]

On a grid of nodes \(\theta_q\), \(q = 1, \dots, Q\), with weights \(w_q\), an irtlib.quadrature.QuadratureGrid, the posterior mass at a node is proportional to \(w_q\,p(\theta_q)\,L(\mathbf{u}\mid\theta_q)\) and the masses \(\tilde{p}(\theta_q \mid \mathbf{u})\) sum to 1. The implementation approximates the integral as

\[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAP}} \approx \sum_{q=1}^{Q}\theta_q\,\tilde{p}(\theta_q \mid \mathbf{u}),\]

with posterior variance

\[\operatorname{Var}(\theta \mid \mathbf{u}) \approx \sum_{q=1}^{Q} (\theta_q-\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAP}})^2 \tilde{p}(\theta_q \mid \mathbf{u}).\]

These are Equations 4 and 5 of Bock and Mislevy [1982, p. 433]; Bock and Mislevy [1982, p. 432] credit the quadrature formulas to Bock and Aitkin [1981]. catsim returns the variance, the square of their Equation 5, without the Sheppard’s correction they apply (p. 433). Like Bock and Mislevy, catsim uses evenly spaced nodes rather than Gauss–Hermite points, which offer little advantage here because the likelihood is not a polynomial (p. 433).

The estimator returns a Estimate with \(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAP}}\) and the posterior standard deviation \(\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(\theta \mid \mathbf{u})}\) as its standard error. With no administered item the posterior is the prior, so the estimate and its standard error are the prior’s mean and standard deviation on the grid.

Computation#

The estimate is irtlib’s, irtlib.ability.expected_a_posteriori. By default the grid has 121 equally spaced nodes on \([-6, 6]\) (spacing 0.1) and the prior is \(\mathcal{N}(0, 1)\). The grid is accurate while it resolves the posterior. Compared with a 2,401-node reference, the default grid reproduces the posterior mean and standard deviation to within about 0.25% while the posterior standard deviation is at least 0.065, which takes a test information of about 240. Below that it degrades: errors reach about 6% at a posterior standard deviation of 0.05 and about 44% at 0.035. The default follows the advice of Bock and Mislevy [1982, p. 433], who suggested about 80 points between \(\pm 4\) standard deviations, also a spacing of 0.1, to keep precision down to a posterior standard deviation of 0.2. A coarser grid degrades sooner: with 41 nodes (spacing 0.3), errors reach about 7% at a posterior standard deviation of 0.15 and about 44% at 0.11. The estimator reports the posterior standard deviation as its standard error, so a precision stopper with a threshold below about 0.065 needs a finer grid than the default.

Behavior and Comparison#

The estimate depends on the prior and on the grid. The default grid (121 nodes on \([-6, 6]\)) is accurate while the posterior standard deviation is at least about 0.065; longer tests need a finer grid (see Computation above).

Comparison with catR

The estimator is catR’s thetaEst with method = "EAP", and its standard error is that of catR’s semTheta, the posterior standard deviation. catR integrates by the trapezoid rule on its parInt grid, by default 33 nodes on \([-4, 4]\), which halves the weights of the two end nodes; catsim’s default grid has 121 equally weighted nodes on \([-6, 6]\) (ADR-0020). A grid with catR’s nodes and weights reproduces catR’s estimator:

import numpy
from catsim.estimation import EAPEstimator
from irtlib.quadrature import QuadratureGrid

weights = numpy.full(33, 8 / 32)
weights[[0, -1]] /= 2
catr_eap = EAPEstimator(grid=QuadratureGrid(nodes=numpy.linspace(-4, 4, 33), weights=weights))

On such grids the parity tests find catR’s estimates and standard errors, to a relative \(10^{-10}\), on fixed responses under normal and uniform priors, on catsim’s 121 nodes as on catR’s 33, and in whole tests on recorded responses. The simulations that check the randomized selectors against catR estimate with this estimator too. They find no departure.

Guarantees#

  1. The estimate is finite for all-correct and all-incorrect responses.

  2. The standard error is the posterior standard deviation on the grid.

  3. Relative to a flatter prior, a centered normal prior produces stronger shrinkage of extreme short-test estimates toward the prior mean.

API Reference#

class catsim.estimation.EAPEstimator(grid: QuadratureGrid | None = None, log_prior: LogPrior | None = None)[source]#

Bases: BaseEstimator

Expected a Posteriori ability estimator.

The mean of the ability’s posterior over a quadrature grid, with the posterior standard deviation as its standard error; see Expected A Posteriori Estimator. The estimator keeps no posterior between calls: it computes a session’s posterior when it is needed.

Parameters:
gridQuadratureGrid or None, optional

irtlib’s quadrature grid over which the posterior is evaluated. Default is QuadratureGrid.uniform(), 121 nodes on [-6, 6].

log_priorLogPrior or None, optional

Vectorized log-density of the prior, such as irtlib’s normal_log_prior or uniform_log_prior. Default is normal_log_prior(), the standard normal.

estimate(context: StepContext) → Estimate[source]#

Return the posterior mean and the posterior standard deviation.

With no administered item they are the prior’s mean and standard deviation on the grid. Each call evaluates the likelihood once per grid node, which is its evaluation count.

Parameters:
contextStepContext

The session to estimate for. Its theta and se are not used.

Returns:
Estimate

The posterior mean of \(\theta\), the posterior standard deviation and the number of grid nodes.

Raises:
ValueError

If the prior is NaN or \(+\infty\) at a node of the grid, or the posterior has no mass at any node: a prior whose support misses the grid, or responses whose likelihood is 0 at every node.

property grid: QuadratureGrid#

Return the quadrature grid used by the estimator.
