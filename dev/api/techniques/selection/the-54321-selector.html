5-4-3-2-1 Selector#

Class:

catsim.selection.The54321Selector

Sources:

McBride and Martin [1983]

Motivation#

Under maximum-information selection every examinee starts from the same ability estimate, so the opening items of every test are the same few items. The 5-4-3-2-1 technique of McBride and Martin [1983] randomizes only those opening items.

Definition#

The opening items are drawn from bins that shrink by one item at each step:

  • the first item is drawn uniformly at random from the 5 most informative items at the current estimate;

  • the second from the 4 most informative remaining items;

  • the third from 3 and the fourth from 2;

  • from the fifth item on, the most informative remaining item is administered.

This is the schedule described by Georgiadou et al. [2007, p. 9] in their review of exposure-control strategies.

With initial_bin_size \(s\), the item at 0-indexed test position \(k\) is drawn from the \(\max(1, s - k)\) most informative items that are neither administered nor excluded, plus any item tied in information with the last of them (ADR-0011: Break Ties Between Items Uniformly at Random).

Parameters#

Parameter

Default

Description

initial_bin_size

5

Number of most informative items the first item is drawn from; each following item draws from one fewer, down to 1.

Behavior and Comparison#

Unlike the randomesque selector, which keeps drawing from a fixed number of items for the whole test [Georgiadou et al., 2007, p. 9], the late items, which matter most for precision, are chosen by maximum information. Magis and Raîche [2012, p. 12] list the technique among the methods that choose at random among several near-optimal items, without defining it.

Guarantees#

  1. It never returns an item that was already administered or excluded.

  2. The item at position \(k\) is drawn with the session’s random generator from the \(\max(1, s - k)\) most informative remaining items.

  3. From position \(s - 1\) on, it returns the most informative remaining item.

  4. When no item remains, it raises NoItemsAvailableError.

  5. It applies no exposure cap: the run’s exposure rates do not change its choice.

API Reference#

class catsim.selection.The54321Selector(initial_bin_size: int = 5)[source]#

Bases: BaseSelector

Draw each opening item at random from a shrinking bin of the most informative items.

The 5-4-3-2-1 method of McBride and Martin [1983]: the first item is drawn from the 5 most informative items at the current estimate, the second from the 4 most informative remaining ones, and so on, until from the fifth item on the most informative item is administered. Items tied with the least informative item of a bin also join it. The selector applies no exposure cap: context.exposure_rates is ignored.

Parameters:
initial_bin_sizeint, optional

Number of most informative items the first item is drawn from; each following item draws from one fewer. Default is 5.

Raises:
TypeError

If initial_bin_size is not an integer.

ValueError

If initial_bin_size is smaller than 1.

Notes

For full algorithmic details, see 5-4-3-2-1 Selector.

Return an item drawn uniformly from the bin of the most informative remaining items.

The bin holds initial_bin_size items at the first position, one fewer at each following position, and at least one, plus every item tied with its least informative item.
