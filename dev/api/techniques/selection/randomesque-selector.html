Randomesque Selector#

Class:

catsim.selection.RandomesqueSelector

Sources:

Kingsbury and Zara [1989]

Motivation#

Maximum-information selection gives examinees of similar ability the same items, so the most informative items of a bank are overexposed. The randomesque technique of Kingsbury and Zara [1989] relaxes maximum-information selection by adding controlled randomness among the most informative items. Magis and Raîche [2012, p. 12] list it among the exposure-control methods that choose at random among several optimal items near the current estimate.

Definition#

At each step the selector ranks the items that are neither administered nor excluded by Fisher information at the current ability estimate, keeps the bin_size most informative of them, and draws one uniformly at random from them. Items tied in information with the last of them also join the set (ADR-0017: Break Ties Between Items Uniformly at Random).

Parameters#

Parameter

Default

Description

bin_size

required

Number of most informative remaining items each item is drawn from, at least 1. A bin of 1 is maximum-information selection.

Behavior and Comparison#

Unlike the 5-4-3-2-1 technique, it keeps drawing from the same number of best items for the whole test rather than switch to maximum information [Georgiadou et al., 2007, p. 9]. The random draw from the candidate set is its only exposure control.

Comparison with catR

The selector is catR’s nextItem with criterion = "MFI" and randomesque set to bin_size; catR also applies randomesque bins to its other criteria, and catsim to maximum information only. The parity tests (ADR-0020) find catR’s bin, the items it draws from, in four contexts, and simulations of 1,000 examinees with bins of 5, on 10-item tests and on tests that stop at a standard error of 0.4 or 20 items, give catR’s bias, RMSE, mean test length, overlap rate and exposure rates within their Monte Carlo error. They find no departure.

Guarantees#

  1. It never returns an item that was already administered or excluded.

  2. It returns one of the bin_size most informative remaining items at the current estimate, or an item tied with the last of them, drawn with the generator of the call.

  3. When no item remains, it raises NoItemsAvailableError.

  4. It applies no exposure cap: the exposure rates do not change its choice.

API Reference#

class catsim.selection.RandomesqueSelector(bin_size: int)[source]#

Bases: BaseSelector

Draw each item at random from the bin_size most informative remaining items.

The randomesque method of Kingsbury and Zara [1989]. Items tied with the least informative item of the bin also join it. The selector applies no exposure cap: context.exposure_rates is ignored.

Parameters:
bin_sizeint

Number of most informative remaining items each item is drawn from, at least 1.

Raises:
TypeError

If bin_size is not an integer.

ValueError

If bin_size is smaller than 1.

Notes

For full algorithmic details, see Randomesque Selector.
