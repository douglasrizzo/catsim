Proportional Selector#

Class:

catsim.selection.ProportionalSelector

Sources:

Segall (2004); Barrada et al. (2008)

Motivation#

The proportional selector uses the same position-dependent schedule as the progressive selector, but rather than blend random and information scores, it samples directly from a probability distribution defined by item information.

That gives the method a clean probabilistic interpretation: fully random at the start of the test and increasingly concentrated on high-information items as the test progresses. At the last item the information is raised to the test length, so the longer the test, the closer its last draws come to maximum-information selection; for the test lengths CATs use, Barrada et al. (2008, p. 498) expect items of low information to be drawn with negligible probability by then.

Definition#

At position \(n\) of a test of planned length \(N\), item \(i\) is selected with probability

\[\Pr(\text{select } i) = \frac{I_i(\hat{\theta})^{h_n}} {\sum_{j \in \mathcal{A}} I_j(\hat{\theta})^{h_n}}, \qquad h_n = N\,w_n,\]

where \(\mathcal{A}\) is the set of available items (the remaining items under the exposure cap, or every remaining item when the cap excludes them all) and \(w_n\) is the progressive selector’s schedule with its own acceleration parameter \(s\), any real number:

\[w_n = \frac{\sum_{m=1}^{n-1} m^{s}}{\sum_{m=1}^{N-1} m^{s}} \quad (n < N), \qquad w_n = 1 \quad (n \ge N).\]

The exponent is 0 at the first position, where selection is uniformly random, and grows to the test length \(N\) at the last; with \(s = 0\) it grows linearly. Past position \(N\) it stays at \(N\).

Segall (2004) selects each item with probability proportional to \(V_i\); in his paper \(V_i\) was an expected variance, and with Fisher information \(V_i = I_i(\hat{\theta})\) (Barrada et al., 2008, p. 497, Equations 8–10). Barrada et al. raise the information to a power that grows during the test (p. 497, Equation 11): a power of 0 gives random selection, a power of 1 Segall’s method, and a large power approaches maximum information (pp. 497–498). Their power is \(h_n\) above (p. 498, Equation 13, read with \(K\) in the denominator as the progressive selector’s page explains for Equation 12).

Computation#

catR computes the power the same way in nextItem.R, as thr times the progressive weight. catsim departs from both where the progressive selector does: the default is \(s = 0\), where catR’s AP defaults to 1; past position \(N\) the exponent stays at \(N\), where catR’s keeps growing; and with \(N = 1\) the exponent is 1, Segall’s method, where Equation 13 and catR give 0.

catsim divides the information by its maximum over the available items before raising it to the power, which leaves the probabilities unchanged and keeps a large exponent from underflowing every weight to zero.

Parameters#

Parameter

Default

Description

test_size

required

Planned test length \(N\), at least 1, which the exponent reaches at the last position.

acceleration

0.0

Acceleration parameter \(s\), any finite number. 0 makes the exponent grow linearly; positive values keep sampling near uniform longer and negative values concentrate it sooner.

r_max

1.0

Maximum exposure rate, in \((0, 1]\). Items at or above it are left out before sampling, unless every remaining item is.

Behavior and Comparison#

At the last item of a 20-item test at \(\theta = 0\), over 20 simulated 500-item 3PL banks, the most informative item is drawn with probability 0.32 to 0.96 (median 0.64).

Guarantees#

  1. It never returns an item that was already administered or excluded.

  2. When test_size is above 1, every available item is equally likely at the first position, whatever the acceleration.

  3. When every available item gives zero information at the estimate, it draws uniformly among them.

  4. When every remaining item is at or above the exposure cap, it samples from all the remaining items.

  5. It draws with the session’s random generator, so a run with the same seed administers the same items.

References#

Barrada, J. R., Olea, J., Ponsoda, V., & Abad, F. J. (2008). Incorporating randomness in the Fisher information for improving item-exposure control in CATs. British Journal of Mathematical and Statistical Psychology, 61(2), 493-513. https://doi.org/10.1348/000711007X230937. Open copy: first author’s page.

API Reference#

class catsim.selection.ProportionalSelector(test_size: int, acceleration: float = 0.0, r_max: float = 1.0)[source]#

Bases: BaseSelector

Proportional selector that samples items with probability proportional to weighted information.

Each available item is drawn with probability proportional to its information raised to a power that follows Barrada et al.’s (2008) schedule from 0 at the first position, where sampling is uniform, to the test length at test_size.

Parameters:
test_sizeint

Planned number of items in the test, at least 1. From that position on, the exponent equals it.

accelerationfloat, optional

Barrada et al.’s acceleration parameter \(s\), any finite number, which shapes the exponent’s growth as \(t\) shapes ProgressiveSelector’s weight. 0 makes the growth linear. Default is 0.

r_maxfloat, optional

Maximum exposure rate for items. Items with exposure rates >= r_max will not be selected unless no other items are available. Default is 1 (no restriction).

Raises:
TypeError

If test_size is not an integer.

ValueError

If test_size is smaller than 1, acceleration is not finite or r_max is not in (0, 1].

Notes

For full algorithmic details, see Proportional Selector.
